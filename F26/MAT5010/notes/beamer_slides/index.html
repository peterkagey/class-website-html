Beamer presentations

2026-10-20

A very common way to make slides for a math presentation is by using a \(\LaTeX\) document class called Beamer. I typically recommend that students use other tools (which we will talk about today) to make mathematical slides, but being able to make Beamer slides is an important skill for a graduate student in mathematics to know.

A Beamer slide show will compile into a PDF, typically with a distinct page for each animation. This means that a single animated "slide" (called a "frame") on your PDF might consist of several pages (called "slides").

Here is a minimal working example of a Beamer presentation, although you will probably want to bring in your favorite packages and commands. 

\documentclass{beamer}
\title{Title}
\author{Name}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{frame}
\frametitle{First slide title}
  This is the first sentence.
  \pause

  The second sentence will appear on the next page of the PDF.
\end{frame}
\end{document}

Basic animation

Since your Beamer presentation compiles to a PDF, the animations are just controlling what appears on each page. You can do this with \pause commands, which incrementally add the text to each frame in a series of slides, as you can see illustrated above.

Add some \pause commands, and/or look up more sophisticated ways of animating your frame, such as overlay specifications.

Choosing colors and themes

You can make your presentation look more interesting by using themes and colors.

  1. Look up themes in the Beamer theme gallery, and include it in your Beamer presentation by placing something like \usetheme{AnnArbor} in your preamble.
  2. Look up color themes in the Beamer color theme gallery, and include one in your Beamer presentation by placing something like \usecolortheme{dolphin} in your preamble.
  3. By default, Beamer includes a navigation panel in the lower-right corner that most people don't use. If you don't plan on using it, remove it with 
    \setbeamertemplate{navigation symbols}{}

Getting ready for your presentation

You'll present something in class for <3 minutes. Here are the requirements:

  1. Make a Beamer presentation consisting of a title slide (frame) plus one or two other frames, including enough content to talk about for a few minutes. Make sure you have give your presentation a \title{...} and included your name as the \author{...}.
  2. At least one (non-title) frame should include an animation.
  3. At least one (non-title) frame should include content in a block or alertblock like this: 
    
        \begin{block}{Title for block}
  Content in block.
\end{block}
  4. Make sure to include some mathematical content both in inline and in display math mode.
  5. Recommended, but not required: put an image, lists, tables, theorems, etc.
  6. Compile your Beamer presentation, download it as a PDF (not as a .tex file), upload it to the class' Overleaf project, and name it FirstNameLastName.pdf. (You can find an invitation to the project on our class' Canvas page.)
  7. Edit main.tex to make sure that there's a line the says \includepdf[pages=-]{FirstNameLastName.pdf} in the order in which you would like to present.
