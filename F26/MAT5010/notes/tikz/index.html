Drawing with TikZ

2026-09-15

You can draw graphs (in the graph theory sense), diagrams, and other illustrations in \(\LaTeX\) with TikZ! This is a huge library, and this page only means to serve a small introduction to it.

  1. Add \usepackage{tikz} to your preamble.
  2. Draw a red circle of radius \(2\) centered at the origin by copy/pasting the following code: 
    \begin{tikzpicture}
  \fill[red] (0,0) circle (2);
\end{tikzpicture}
  3. Draw a green disk that is tangent to the red disk.
  4. Draw a white disk with a blue boundary that is tangent to both disks by replacing \fill with \draw.
  5. You can draw a sequence of line segments, the first from \((1,2)\) to \((2,4)\) and the second from \((2,4)\) to \((2,2)\) with the following: 
    \draw (1,2) -- (2,4) -- (2,2);
    Use this to draw a triangle whose vertices are at the center of the circles.

PGFPlots

The pgfplots package is based on TikZ and allows you to include plots in native \(\LaTeX\), rather than exporting plots from other software and including it in your document via \includegraphics (which is typically what I do instead!)

  1. Add \usepackage{pgfplots} and \pgfplotsset{compat=1.18} to your preamble. You can remove the TikZ package if you want because it's included.
  2. Copy and paste this minimal working example, and then change some parameters to see what happens. If you make the number of samples too big, the document may take a very long time to compile. 
    \begin{tikzpicture}
  \begin{axis}[xlabel={$x$},ylabel={$y$},]
    \addplot[domain=-5:5, samples=100, red]{x^2};
  \end{axis}
\end{tikzpicture}
