Figure placement

2026-09-15

In \(\LaTeX\) the default figures float, meaning they move to wherever the compiler guesses they fit best, while juggling several typographic concerns. If you have an opinion about where you want your float to go, you're welcome to express it with specifiers such as h, t, b, p, !, and H, which you can read about what these mean in the Overleaf docs. Even if you express your opinion, \(\LaTeX\) might not do what you tell it to do.

Place a figure somewhere in the middle of your document, and then see how it moves around as you use different specifiers: 
\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{example_image.png}
  \caption{Enter Caption}
  \label{fig:placeholder}
\end{figure}

A non-floating figure

Rarely, you will want a figure that doesn't float. However, before you go about forcing it somewhere, it is worth considering whether or not this should be a figure with a caption at all! Occasionally it makes sense to put an illustration directly in the text without referring to it as a figure.

If you decide that indeed you want the figure labeled and captioned, what I usually do is avoid using the figure environment altogether. Instead, I use the captions package, and then use a minipage environment: 

\noindent
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{image.png}
  \captionof{figure}{Caption goes here.}
\end{minipage}

Be careful, however, because a floating figure could interact with this one in such a way that Figure 2 appears before Figure 1 in your document.
