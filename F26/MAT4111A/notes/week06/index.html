Calculus

Many Putnam problems—especially A1,A2,B1,B2—depend on clever algebraic manipulations or applications of multivariable calculus concepts.

Completing the square

We can write every monic quadratic polynomial \(x^2 + ax + b\) in the form \( (x + c)^2 + d\), where \(c = a/2\) and \(b = c^2 + d\).

Cylindrical and spherical coordinates

Recall that we can write points in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) in polar coordinates \[ \begin{align*} x &= r \cos(\theta) \\ y &= r \sin(\theta). \end{align*} \] When evaluating a double integral in polar coordinates \(dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta\).

We can write points in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) in cylindrical coordinates as \[ \begin{align*} x &= r \cos(\theta) \\ y &= r \sin(\theta) \\ z &= z. \end{align*} \] When evaluating a triple integral in cylindrical coordinates the integrand is written \(dx\,dy\,dz = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz.\)

We can write points in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) in spherical coordinates as \[ \begin{align*} x &= \rho\cos(\theta)\sin(\phi) \\ y &= \rho\sin(\theta)\sin(\phi) \\ z &= \rho \cos(\phi). \end{align*} \] When evaluating a triple integral in spherical coordinates \[ dx\,dy\,dz = \rho^2\sin(\phi)\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta. \]
