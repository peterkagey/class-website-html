Look over the solutions to the week 5 and week 6 quizzes. For each quiz, write a sentence or two about a mistake or misconception you had, and clarify how to fix it.
  1. The quiz from Tuesday of week 5 asked you about a binary operation on cosets being well-defined.
    Answer key.
    1. Let \(a = r^2\), \(a' = r^2f\), and \(b = b' = r\). Then \((r^2 * r)H = H \neq (r^2f * r) H = rfH \), since \(rfH = \{rf, r\} \neq H\).
    2. If \(G = D_4\), then \(r^2\) commutes with the generators, since \(f * r^2 = r^2f \) and \(r * r^2 = r^2 * r \), and therefore \(r^2\) commutes with all of \(G\). Since \(e\) also commutes with all of the elements of \(G\), we can see that everything in \(N = \{e, r^2\} \) commutes with each element of \(G\).
      If \(a' = a * n_1\) and \(b' = b * n_2\) for some \(n_1, n_2 \in N\) then \[ a' * b' = (a * n_1 * b * n_2) = (a * b * n_1 * n_2) \in (a * b)N. \]
  2. The quiz from Thursday of week 5, which was given to the morning section on Tuesday of week 6 asked you to show that a subgroup is not normal or to show that \(\langle r^n \rangle \triangleleft D_{2n}\).
    Answer key.
    1. We can see that \(H = \langle r^2f \rangle = \{e, r^2f\}\) is not normal in \(D_3\) by conjugating \(r^2f\) by \(r\) \[ r * r^2f * r^{-1} = fr^{-1} = rf \not\in H. \]
    2. In \(D_{2n}\), we can check that \(r^n\) is in its own conjugacy class because \(r^nf = fr^{2n-n} = fr^n\), and thus \[ \begin{align*} r^k * r^n * r^{-k} &= r^{k+n-k} \\ &= r^n, \\ r^kf * r^n * (r^kf)^{-1} &= r^{n+k}f * r^kf \\ &= r^{n+k}f*fr^{2n-k} \\ &= r^n. \end{align*} \]
  3. The quiz from Thursday of week 6 asked you to construct an isomorphism \(\phi\colon D_2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times C_2\).
    Answer key.

    When you're learning these ideas, one of the best ways of getting started is by trying to draw two Cayley diagrams and "match them up."

    In this particular case, you will find that any bijection works, as long as you send the identity to the identity. \[ \begin{align*} \phi(e) &= (0, e)\\ \phi(r) &= (1, e)\\ \phi(f) &= (1, r)\\ \phi(rf) &= (0, r). \end{align*} \] This is clearly a bijection, and we check that it is structure preserving by checking the relations on \(D_2\) are preserved: \[ \begin{align*} \phi(r)\star\phi(r) &= (1, e) \star (1, e) = (1 + 1, e * e) = (0, e) \\ \phi(f)\star\phi(f) &= (1, r) \star (1, r) = (1 + 1, r * r) = (0, e) \\ (\phi(r)\star\phi(f))^2 &= ((1, e) \star (1, r))^2 = (0, r)^2 = (0, e). \\ \end{align*} \]
