Choice Exercise 6 [≤2] (Quiz review - TODO) Look over the solutions to the week 7 quiz. Write a sentence or two about a mistake or misconception you had and clarify how to fix it.

The quiz from Thursday of week 7 asked you about conjugacy under homomorphism.

Answer key.

Since \(g\) and \(g'\) are conjugate, there exists some \(\ell \in L\) such that \[ \ell \star g \star \ell^{-1} = g', \] and since \(\phi\) is structure preserving, \[ \phi(\ell \star g \star \ell^{-1}) = \phi(\ell) \bullet \phi(g) \bullet \phi(\ell^{-1}). \] We showed on Tuesday that if \(h = \phi(\ell)\), then \(h^{-1} = \phi(\ell^{-1})\), and thus \(\phi(g')\) is \(\phi(g)\) conjugated by \(h = \phi(\ell)\).

Choice Exercise 8 [≤10] (TODO - normal subgroups are not transitive)

Prove that the normal subgroup relation \(H \triangleleft G\) is not transitive by doing the following.

  1. On Exam 1, you proved that $A_n \triangleft S_n$. Repeat this argument here. (Prove the homework 1 hint!)
  2. On HW 5? you proved that conjugation in the symmetric group preserves cycle structure. Show that \(N = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}\) is a subgroup and is closed under conjugation. (It's closed under conjugation by elements of \(S_n\), so it's definitely closed by conjugation under \(A_n\).) And thus \(N \triangleleft S_4\) and \(H \triangleleft A_4\).
  3. Prove that \(H \cong C_2 \times C_2\) by describing an isomorphism, and briefly say why \(H\) is abelian.
  4. Quickly recall the proof that all subgroups of an abelian group are normal subgroups, and give an example of a proper non-trivial subgroup of \(H \triangleleft N\).
  5. Prove that \(H\) is not a normal subgroup of \(S_4\), and explain why this implies that the normal subgroup relation is not transitive.
Choice Exercise 9 [?] ⭐️ (Circle symmetries) Do the following exercises about quotienting $(\mathbb{R},+)$.
  1. Prove that the set of rotation matrices \[ SO_2(\mathbb{R}) = \left\{\begin{pmatrix}\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{pmatrix}\middle|\, \theta \in \mathbb{R} \right\} \] forms a group under matrix multiplication.
  2. Prove that the map $\phi \colon \mathbb{R} \to SO_2(\mathbb{R})$ given by \[ \phi(x) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi x) & -\sin(2\pi x) \\ \sin(2\pi x) & \cos(2\pi x) \end{pmatrix} \] is a homomorphism but not an isomorphism.
  3. Determine the kernel and image of $\phi$.
  4. What group is $\mathbb{R}/\ker(\phi)$ isomorphic to?
