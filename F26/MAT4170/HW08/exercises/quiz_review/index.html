The quiz from Thursday of week 9 asked you to prove that in a ring \((R, +, \times)\), \( -a \times -b = a \times b. \)
Answer key.

The proposition states that \(-(a \times b) = -a \times b.\) We will use this to that \(a \times b\) and \(-a \times -b\) have the same additive inverse, and thus are equal: \begin{align*} (-a \times -b) + -(a \times b) &= (-a \times -b) + (-a \times b) \\ &= -a \times (-b + b) \\ &= -a \times 0_R \\ &= 0_R. \end{align*} Adding \(a \times b\) to the first and last expression in the above chain of equations yields \begin{align*} -a \times -b = a \times b. \end{align*}
