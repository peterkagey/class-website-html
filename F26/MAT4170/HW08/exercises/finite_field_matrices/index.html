It turns out that many (but not all) of the theorems that you learned about vector spaces over \(\mathbb{R}\) in linear algebra also work over finite fields.

  1. Let \(\mathbb{Z}_5^{3 \times 3}\) be the ring of \(3 \times 3\) matrices over the field \(\mathbb{Z}_5\). Compute the determinant \(\det(A) \in \mathbb{Z}_5\) where \[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \]
  2. Because the determinant of \(A\) is nonzero, the matrix is invertible. Adapt the standard matrix inversion techniques to compute the inverse matrix \(A^{-1} \in \mathbb{Z}_5^{3 \times 3}\), which should have entries that are integers mod \(5\).
  3. Compute \(\det(A^{-1})\) and show that \(\det(A^{-1}) = \det(A)^{-1}\), where the inverse is with respect to multiplication mod \(5\).
