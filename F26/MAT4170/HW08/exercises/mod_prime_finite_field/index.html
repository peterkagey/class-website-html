Let \(\mathbb{Z}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\) where \(p\) is prime, and let \(a \in \mathbb{Z}_p\) be a nonzero element.
  1. Prove the following three facts:
    1. Explain how you know that \(p\) does not divide \(a\), and thus \(p\) does not divide \(a^k\) for any positive integer \(k\).
    2. Since \(\{a, a^2, \dots \} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_p\) is finite, there must be some pair of positive integers \(k > \ell\) such that \(a^k \equiv a^\ell \pmod p\) and thus \[ a^k - a^\ell \equiv a^{\ell}(a^{k-\ell} - 1) \equiv 0 \pmod p \] Explain why \(a^{\ell} \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\), and thus \(a^{k-\ell} \equiv 1 \pmod p \).
    3. Prove that there exists a positive integer \(b\) such that \(a^b = 1 \in \mathbb{Z}_p\). (Hint: this is one sentence!)
  2. Show why the above argument implies that in the ring \((\mathbb{Z}_p,+,\times)\), all nonzero elements have a multiplicative inverse, and thus forms a field.
  3. Show that if \(n \geq 2\) is not prime, then \((\mathbb{Z}_n,+,\times)\) is not a field.
