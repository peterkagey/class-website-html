Normal subgroups

Today we will explore normal subgroups, which are subgroups which inherit a well-defined operation on their cosets.

We say that a subgroup $N \leq (G,*)$ is a normal subgroup denoted $N \triangleleft G$ if for all \(g \in G\) and \(n \in N\), \[g*n*g^{-1} \in N.\]
Essentially the only reason we care about this definition is because normal subgroups are precisely the subgroups we can quotient by.
Let \((G,*)\) be a group and \(H \leq H\). The operation on cosets \[ aH \circledast bH = (a * b)H \] is well defined if and only if \(H\) is a normal subgroup.
Contrapositive

Suppose that the operation on cosets is well-defined, meaning that for all \(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2 \in G\), if \(a_1H = a_2H\) and \(b_1H = b_2H\) then \[(a_1 * b_1)H = (a_2 * b_2)H.\]

Let \(h\) be an arbitrary element of \(H\), let \(g\) be an arbitrary element of \(G\), and then let \(a_1 = g\), \(a_2 = a_1 * h\) and \(b_1 = b_2 = g^{-1}\). By the assumption that \(\circledast\) is well-defined, there exists \(h_0 \in H\) such that \[ \begin{align*} a_1 * b_1 &= a_2 * b_2 * h_0 \\ g * g^{-1} &= (g * h) * g^{-1} * h_0 \\ h_0^{-1} &= g * h * g^{-1}. \end{align*} \] Since \(H\) is closed under inverses, \(g * h * g^{-1} \in H\) for all \(g \in G\) and \(h \in H\), and thus \(H \triangleleft G\).

Converse

Assume that \(H \triangleleft G\). Suppose that \(a_1H = a_2H\) and \(b_1H = b_2H\) for \(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2 \in G\), and thus there exists \(h_1, h_2 \in H\) such that \(a_1 = a_2h_1\) and \(b_1 = b_2h_2\).

Using the conjugation assumption with \(g = b_2^{-1}\) and \(h = h_1\), let \[ \begin{align*} h' &= g * h * g^{-1} = b_2^{-1} * h_1 * b_2 \in H \\ b_2 * h' &= h_1 b_2. \end{align*} \] Then using this substitution, \[ \begin{align*} a_1 * b_1 &= (a_2 * h_1) * (b_2 * h_2) \\ &= (a_2 * b_2) * (h_3 * h_2) \in (a_2 * b_2)H, \end{align*} \] therefore \((a_1 * b_1)H = (a_2 * b_2)H\) and \(\circledast\) is well-defined.
