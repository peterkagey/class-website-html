Proofs portfolio

(Due on 2026-11-13 on Gradescope)

Your proofs portfolio exists principally as an opportunity to show off your best work from this class and to practice revisiting and revising your proof writing in this class.

There are two main approaches you can take with your proofs portfolio. Your first option (which I recommend) is to make something that is uniquely yours—tying together some of your favorite examples, exercise write-ups, and proofs from the term. Your other option is to be encyclopedic and write a document that discusses many topics from this course at a shallow level.

Now that I'm more than ten years on from having graduated college, I love whenever I stumble across old college work in my drawers or on my hard drive. When choosing how to write this, I encourage you to be guided by what the future version of you would most like to see.

Requirements

Go through this checklist to make sure your proofs portfolio satisfies the following.

  1. The proofs portfolio must be 4–6 pages with ten point font (i.e., \documentclass[10pt]{article}) and 1 inch margins (i.e., \usepackage[margin=1in]{geometry}.)
  2. The portfolio should have at least two propositions, two proofs, two definitions, and two examples, but potentially many more, depending on their length.
  3. You should be including the work you've done this term that you're proudest of, that you found the most interesting, or that you felt you understood the most deeply. Try to make it so that your portfolio doesn't look like anyone else's.
  4. Like all good mathematical writing, the portfolio should not only consist of propositions/proofs/definitions/examples, but also motivating text between these environments which tells the reader why you care and why they should care. (In this portfolio, you are encouraged to make it personal: share anecdotes of where you had "aha" moments, got stuck, misunderstood the proof, were proud of an idea, etc.)
  5. You should solicit feedback on every part of the portfolio, ideally from a classmate, but alternatively from me or from someone else who is familiar with the subject matter. (In particular, what you've included in your homework submission only counts as having being reviewed by me if you asked for feedback on a specific section.)
