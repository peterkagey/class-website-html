Exercise 2 [4] (Generated subgroups are the smallest subgroups)

Let \(G\) be a group and suppose that \(g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n \in G\). Recall that we defined \(\langle g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n\rangle\) to be the set of words consisting of letters in \(\langle g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n\rangle\). Prove that this is the smallest subgroup of \(G\) containing \(g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n\).

Exercise 2 [4] (Cyclic subgroup practice)

Given a group \(G,\star\) and an element \(g \in G\), we say that the cyclic subgroup generated by \(g\) is \[ \langle g \rangle = \{ g^k \mid k \in \mathbb{Z} \}, \] the group of words that can be written in terms of \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\) under \(\star\), the same operation as the parent group.

We can generate other subgroups, and we use an analogous notation. Given \(g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n \in G\), the subgroup \(\langle g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n \rangle\) consists of all of the words that consist of these generators and their inverses.

  1. Let \(G = D_6\), the dihedral group of the hexagon.
    1. Write all of the elements in the subgroup \(\langle rf, r^3f \rangle\).
    2. Write all of the elements in the subgroup \(\langle r^2, f \rangle\).
  2. Recall that a cyclic group (or cyclic subgroup) is a group (respectively subgroup) that can be generated by a single element. The direct product \(C_2 \times C_3\) does not look like a cyclic group, but it is! Find a single generator \(g \in C_2 \times C_3\) such that \[ C_2 \times C_3 = \langle g \rangle \]
  3. Find the order of the largest cyclic subgroup in \(S_5\), and give an example of an element that generates it.
  4. We saw that the dihedral group of the square \(D_4\) cannot be generated by a single element (i.e. \(D_4\) is not cyclic) but it can be generated by two elements: \(D_4 = \langle r, f \rangle\). Find all pairs of elements \(g_1, g_2 \in D_4\) such that \(D_4 = \langle g_1, g_2 \rangle\).
  5. Prove or disprove the following: if \(G\) is a cyclic group, and \(H \leq G\) is a subgroup of \(G\), then \(H\) must also be a cyclic group. (Hint: check in your head that your argument works both when \(G\) is a finite group, and when \(G = \mathbb{Z}\).)
