Historically, \(i = \sqrt{-1}\) was "invented" to determine roots of polynomials in \(\mathbb{R}[x]\) such as \(x^2 + 4x + 5\), which has roots at \(-2 \pm i\). We're going to do a similar construction starting with a finite field.

  1. Verify that no elements of \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) are roots of the polynomial \(x^2 + x + 1 \in \mathbb{Z}_2[x]\).
  2. Let \(\alpha \not\in \mathbb{Z}_2\) be a root of the polynomial \(x^2 + x + 1\), and explain why \(\alpha^2 = \alpha + 1\).
  3. Is \(\alpha + 1\) a root of \(x^2 + x + 1 \in \mathbb{Z}_2[x]\)?
  4. Consider the ring \[ \mathbb{Z}_2[\alpha] = \{x + y\alpha \mid x, y \in \mathbb{Z}_2\}, \] show that it is a field, and make a Cayley table for \((\mathbb{Z}_2[\alpha] \setminus \{0\}, \times)\).
  5. Now let's build a bigger field! Prove that every quadratic polynomial in \(\mathbb{Z}_2[x]\) has roots in \(\mathbb{Z}_2[\alpha]\), perhaps by checking all four of them.
  6. Give a cubic polynomial in \(\mathbb{Z}_2[x]\) which does not have any roots in \(\mathbb{Z}_2[\alpha]\).
  7. Let \(\beta\) be a root of this polynomial. Determine how many elements are in \(\mathbb{Z}_2[\beta]\), and prove that \(\mathbb{Z}_2[\beta]\) forms a field and that its group of units is cyclic.
  8. Is there an element of \(\mathbb{Z}_2[\beta]\) that is a root of the polynomial \(x^2 + x + 1\)? How does \(\beta\) relate to \(\alpha\)?
