We can embed the dihedral group $D_n$ into $S_n$. For example, if we mark the corners of the square $1$, $2$, $3$, and $4$, then rotating/reflecting the square permutes these numbers.

  1. [2] Write down the eight permutations in $S_4$ (in cycle notation) corresponding to the eight symmetries of the square, and call this subgroup \(H\).
  2. [3] We can also write this in terms of the generators of a subgroup. Find two elements $x, y \in S_4$ such that $H = \langle x ,y \rangle$, and use these generators to draw a Cayley diagram of \(H\).
  3. [2] Cayley's theorem states that we can embed any finite group $G$ into $S_N$ for large enough some $N$. Show that we can embed \(C_6\) in \(S_5\) by finding a cyclic subgroup of order \(6\) in \(S_5\).
