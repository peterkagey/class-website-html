In this exercise you will work on a problem that appeared on a Putnam Exam, widely considered to be one of the hardest math exams, with a typical median score of 0. Set a timer, and spend at least 20 minutes working on this problem, then look up a solution, understand it, and write it up in your own words.

Let \(*\) be a commutative and associative binary operation on a set $S$. Assume that for every $x$ and $y$ in $S$, there exists $z$ in $S$ such that $x * z = y$. (This $z$ may depend on $x$ and $y$.) Show that if $a,b,c$ are in $S$ and $a*c = b*c$, then $a=b$.

2012 Putnam, Problem A2
