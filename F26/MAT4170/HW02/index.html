Exercise 3 [5] (Cayley diagram) You are encouraged to do this problem on paper or a tablet, and include a photo/screenshot following the instructions in HW 1 Exercise 8.10.
  1. Suppose we have a coin with two states (heads and tails) and thus two symmetries (identity and flip). We call this group \(C_2\), the cyclic group of order \(2\). Draw a Cayley diagram for \(C_2\).
  2. Draw a Cayley diagram for \(D_3\) (the dihedral group of the triangle) using the generators \(r\) and \(r^2f\).
  3. Suppose that we have a coin and a triangle on the table, and we consider this as a system. We call this the direct product of \(C_2\) and \(D_3\) and denote it \({C_2 \times D_3}\).
    We describe each symmetries as a pair \((g_1, g_2)\), where \(g_1 \in C_2\) tells us what we do to the coin, and \(g_2\in D_3\) tells us what we do to the triangle. For instance, if \(c\) means "flip the coin", then we have the following symmetries:
    • flip the coin and rotate the triangle by \(120^\circ\), which we denote \((c, r)\); or
    • we could not flip the coin, but flip the triangle horizontally, which we denote \((e_1, f)\); or
    • we flip the coin, but do nothing to the triangle, which we denote \((c, e_2)\); or ...
    1. How many states does this system have? (i.e. how many distinct symmetries does it have?)
    2. Draw a Cayley diagram illustrating how to generate \(C_2 \times D_3\) with the following three symmetries:
      • flip the coin, and do nothing to the triangle \((c, e_2)\),
      • do nothing to the coin, and rotate the triangle \((e_1, r)\),
      • do nothing to the coin, and flip the triangle horizontally \((e_1, f)\).
    3. Can \( C_2 \times D_3 \) be generated with just two generators? If so, state which two and use them to draw a Cayley diagram. If not, why not?

Choice Exercise 6 [≤6] (Symmetric group practice)

Do any subset of the following for 1 point each:

  1. Let $\pi = (1\ 5\ 3\ 8\ 4\ 6) \in S_9$, explain why $|\pi| = 6,$ where $|\pi|$ is the order of the element $\pi$. Moreover, explain how you know that if a cycle has length $k$ then it has order $k$.
  2. Let $\pi' = (1\ 5)(3\ 8\ 4) \in S_9$, prove that the order of $\pi' = 6$.
  3. Look up what it means for two cycles to be disjoint. In particular, are $(1\ 5\ 9\ 7)$ and $(3\ 8\ 4)$ disjoint? Are $(1\ 5\ 9\ 8)$ and $(3\ 8\ 4)$ disjoint?
  4. Explain why \[(1\ 5\ 9\ 7)(3\ 8\ 4) = (3\ 8\ 4)(1\ 5\ 9\ 7),\] but \[(1\ 5\ 9\ 8)(3\ 8\ 4) \neq (3\ 8\ 4)(1\ 5\ 9\ 8).\] Next, explain how you know that \[ \begin{align} \left((1\ 5\ 9\ 7)(3\ 8\ 4)\right)^7 &= (1\ 5\ 9\ 7)^7(3\ 8\ 4)^7 \\ &= (1\ 5\ 9\ 7)^{3}(3\ 8\ 4)^1 \\ &= (1\ 7\ 9\ 5)(3\ 8\ 4). \end{align} \]
  5. Give an example of a $3$-cycle and a $4$-cycle whose product is a $2$-cycle.
  6. Suppose that we write $\sigma = c_1 c_2 \dots c_n$ as a product of disjoint cycles. Prove that the order of sigma $|\sigma| \leq \operatorname{LCM}(|c_1|,|c_2|,\dots,|c_n|)$, where $|c_i|$ is the order (equivalently length) of the cycle, and use this to compute the order of \[ \tau = (8\ 2)(1\ 9\ 4)(3\ 7\ 5\ 6) \in S_9. \]

Choice Exercise 6 [≤15] (programming)
  1. [3] Prove the following facts about the set \( B_3 \) of $3 \times 3$ matrices that have one $\pm 1$ per row and column.
    1. The identity matrix is in \(B_3\)
    2. If we multiply two matrices in \(B_3\), the result is in \(B_3\).
    3. For every matrix \(A \in B_3\), its inverse is also in the set: \(A^{-1} \in B_3\).
  2. [1] Without looking it up, compute the number of elements in \( B_3 \). (We call this the order of the group.)
  3. [2] Download OpenSCAD, write a module named blob that makes something asymmetrical. You can use any tools that you would like to learn how to do this, but just say which tools you used. Here's my blob, which I display by typing it below the module definition. 
    module blob() {

}

blob();
