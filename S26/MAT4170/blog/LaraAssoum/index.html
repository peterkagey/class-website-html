1.1 Introduction

  • Genetic code and its structure

    • The system of where living cells translate information into protein
    • Consists of 4 nucleotides :
    • DNA : A, T, C, G

    • There are 64 possible codons
    • Codons are meant to start / stop signals
    • If there are multiple codons, then it is the same amino acid

1.3 Contrasting Genetic Code vs. Permutation Groups

1.3.1. Genetic Code

The main purpose of the genetic code is to produce proteins for us, something we all need. Although it is essential, the genetic system is not structured to where it is “perfect”. That is where they differ from permutation groups. Mutations do happen randomly which makes it hard for us to predict the genetic code. While some genetic codes may follow patterns, the rest could be affected by several factors. Genetics are also something that is not stable. They are always evolving and changing, especially over time. You can inherit new traits over time because mutations are capable of altering genetic sequences. With that being said, a mathematical system is not considered to be as flexible as the genetic code.

1.3.2 Permutation Groups

Permutation groups are completely derived and made up from mathematical concepts and ideas. There is a format that they have to hold and rules that they obey. Unlike genetic codes, mathematical concepts, in this case permutation groups, do not change over time. They are considered to be stable, but what could change is perhaps the way a mathematical concept is explained. Unlike genetics, permutation groups are also predictable. Each operation is followed with the same logic and it is also very consistent. It is never random, you expect what will happen and what the result may look like. Permutation groups are mainly used to focus on symmetry and also structure. They can describe the rearrangements of objects which makes them very useful in both science and math. With that being said, in comparison to the genetic code, permutation groups are much more controlled and structured.

Conclusion

In conclusion, although the genetic group and permutation groups come from completely different worlds, even subjects, they still happen to share many similarities. It goes to show how we can connect many different things to mathematical concepts. Both topics focus on how they are structurally formed and systemed, follow rules, include symmetry and also transformations. With all the similarities both the genetic code and permutation groups had, they also had many differences. The genetic code is a biological system that can be random and unpredictable. Permutation groups seem to be more consistent and also predictable. That’s the beauty between Biology, the study of things that exist, versus Math, the study of things we find and later prove.
